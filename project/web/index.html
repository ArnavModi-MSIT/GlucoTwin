What do these dataset names mean?
REPLACE-BG (RBG)
A study of continuous glucose monitoring in adults with type 1 diabetes. RBG is the dataset label used in this project; BG means blood glucose. The replay uses glucose readings and enrollment age/sex. Study description
CGMacros
The dataset name is “CGMacros: a scientific dataset for personalized nutrition and diet monitoring.” It contains glucose, food macronutrients (carbohydrate, protein and fat), activity and clinical information. This replay uses clinical profiles, glucose and heart rate. Dataset description
CGM · HR
CGM means continuous glucose monitoring: repeated glucose readings from a wearable sensor. HR means heart rate.
Synthetic demo
Fictional profiles and sensor readings generated to demonstrate the dashboard.
Import a patient CSV

Use anonymized or synthetic readings only. Files are sent to this localhost server, held in memory and never added to research splits or training. Reloading removes the import catalog; reimport the same file to reopen it.

Header: timestamp,glucose. Use ordered ISO timestamps without a timezone suffix, one five-minute phase, blank glucose for missing readings, and a duration of 1 hour–31 days. Gaps are kept missing. RBG transfer additionally needs 24 hours of history and readings on minutes 00, 05, 10, etc. Conversion from mmol/L uses ×18.

    Blue: historical median. Dark band: 25th–75th percentile. Light band: 10th–90th percentile. Gaps indicate insufficient comparable days. These bands describe past readings, not future predictions.

    How recurring periods are identified

    Each six-hour period needs all 72 expected five-minute slots and at least 80% observed readings. A high period has at least 20% of observed readings above 180 mg/dL; a low period has at least 5% below 70. A pattern repeats when it appears on at least two days and at least half of comparable days. These descriptive rules do not predict the next three days.
